03 · The Concept · Geometric Definitions

What we are looking for.

Each pattern is defined by strict geometric rules before any model is trained: peaks, troughs, necklines and breakout conditions. This is what the analyst scans charts for, and what our rule-based labeller encodes as ground truth.

Bearish Reversal

Double Top

Two peaks at roughly equal price separated by a trough. Confirmed when price closes below the neckline.
Bullish Reversal

Double Bottom

Two troughs at roughly equal price separated by a peak. Confirmed when price closes above the neckline.
Bearish Reversal

Head & Shoulders

Three peaks with a higher middle peak (the head) flanked by two shoulders at roughly equal height.
Bullish Reversal

Inverse Head & Shoulders

Three troughs with a lower middle trough (the head) flanked by two shoulders at roughly equal depth.
Academic Reference For Definitions

Wan & Si (2017) formalise chart-pattern specifications and present them as a reference model for unambiguous definitions in financial time series. That is the literature basis for treating these four shapes as rule-based labels rather than subjective sketches. DOI: 10.1016/j.ins.2017.05.028

Ingestion & cleansing

  1. Session filter

    NYSE regular session only · 09:30–16:00 ET.

  2. Timezone audit

    America/Denver → America/New_York. DST-checked.

  3. Duplicate & NaN removal

    Three quality reports written to disk.

  4. 1-min → 15-min resample

    Microstructure noise swamps geometry at 1-min. 15-min = ~26 bars/session, enough for multi-peak patterns.

  5. Nadaraya-Watson smoothing

    Causal kernel regression, AICc bandwidth. Keeps structure, cuts noise.

05 · Deep Learning Approach

Why deep learning and which models.

Why deep learning here

  • Sequential structure. Patterns are defined over time. Classical models lose ordering.
  • Non-linear geometry. Rules combine bars in non-additive ways. Linear models underfit.
  • Feature learning. No hand-crafted features — the model learns its own.
  • Scalable. One architecture generalises from SPY to any instrument.
Operationally, missed opportunities matter too. Because this is a screening workflow, not auto-execution, selection stays recall-aware with a 0.50 precision floor so noisy patterns do not flood the analyst queue.

Models we trialled

Champion · all 4 patterns in Stage-A run
TCN

Temporal Convolutional Network. Dilated causal convolutions — wide receptive field, no future leakage, fast training. Bai et al. 2018 · arXiv:1803.01271

Baseline · sequence
LSTM

Gated recurrence. Classic financial sequence baseline — tells us whether TCN's extra architecture is earning its keep. Hochreiter & Schmidhuber 1997

06 · Training & Split

Trained, split and tuned.

Time-ordered split · embargo gaps

Random splitting leaks future into past. In the Stage-A run we split strictly in time order with an enforced purge gap between train, validation and test, so the model never learns from future windows by accident.

Parameter tuning

  • Epochs Up to 50, early stop patience 8
  • Optimiser AdamW · learning rate 0.0003 · weight decay 0.0001
  • Batch 64 · reproducible prototype profile
  • Loss Weighted BCE · class-frequency weighting
  • HP search Bayesian/TPE hyperparameter search on validation
  • Threshold Per-pattern, locked on val
  • Selection Recall-aware champion selection with precision floor 0.50
  • Calibration Platt scaling on sequence-model probabilities
  • Normalisation Per-window min-max (price-drift safe)
07 · Detection · How It Works

Sliding windows and locked thresholds.

The model does not look at the whole price history at once. It slides a fixed-length window across the series and classifies each window independently.

  • The amber boxes

    Each box is one sliding window. The configured lookback is 80 bars; this diagram only sketches a few example windows. That span is long enough to capture multi-swing reversal geometry on 15-minute data.

  • The grey line

    Nadaraya–Watson smoothed price. Both labeller and model see this.

  • The teal dot

    Positive classification. Fires only above the per-pattern threshold locked on val.

  • Why this matters

    Thousands of windows scanned per day. Analyst only sees what clears threshold.

10 · Risks & Ethics

What could go wrong.

Technical risks & mitigations

Regime
Regime drift after deployment

Time-based splits, embargoes, and retraining against fresh periods reduce the chance that the model only memorises one market phase.

Support
Pattern quality diverges

The same pipeline performs differently by pattern. In Stage-A, head & shoulders stays recall-heavy but noisy, so we rank alerts by pattern quality instead of treating every signal equally.

Leakage
Future-bar leakage

Time-based splits, enforced purge gaps, causal smoothing and locked validation thresholds keep train/val/test boundaries auditable.

Imbalance
Extreme class imbalance

Weighted BCE, train-only positive augmentation, 1:3 negative downsampling, calibration and support gating keep rare events usable without leaking into test.

Ethical principles

Transparency
Every output is explainable

Gallery, rule trace and real SHAP artifacts sit behind each alert. That gives analysts and regulators a review path instead of a black box.

Accountability
Human-in-the-loop

Decision-support, not autonomous agent. Analyst owns the call.

Fairness
Bias declared

Single-instrument and temporal-regime bias acknowledged, not hidden.

Societal
Herding & alert fatigue

Human review remains mandatory, thresholds stay locked, and the pilot is judged on false-positive control and analyst adoption rather than alert volume alone.

11 · Recommendation · Proven Now vs Next

Start with trust, then scale what earns it.

Do not automate the trade. Productize the decision. The strongest commercial path is a focused analyst workflow first: faster screening, clearer audit trails, and scale only where the signal keeps proving itself.
